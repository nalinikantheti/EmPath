How GeoAI creates the tree-adjacency layer

The AI identifies tree-covered pixels in aerial imagery. Spatial analysis then turns those predictions into scores on existing OpenStreetMap paths.

  1. Get the images — data. Use georeferenced USDA NAIP aerial photographs from 2022 at 60 cm resolution.
  2. Divide the area — preparation. Process the four-ZIP pilot in 505 chunks, with surrounding image context at their edges.
  3. Predict trees — the AI step. Apply the pretrained Restor TCD SegFormer-B0 neural network. It estimates which pixels contain tree cover.
  4. Map the predictions — GIS. Retain the coordinates and turn predicted tree pixels into mapped tree polygons.
  5. Score existing paths — GIS. Measure the predicted tree fraction inside a 5 m corridor around each walking segment. Orange means fewer nearby trees; dark green means more.
  6. Use the scores — analysis. Compare distance and tree-adjacency route objectives. The block walking-friction index averages normalized surface temperature, traffic, and canopy deficit using explicitly chosen equal weights.

What the examples show: the input image, the AI tree prediction, and the resulting path scores. They illustrate the process in one small area per ZIP; the completed analysis covers the entire four-ZIP pilot.

Interpretation. GeoAI uses Restor TCD SegFormer-B0 on USDA NAIP 2022 aerial imagery at 60 cm. Its local prediction accuracy has not been independently validated. Tree adjacency is not measured shade or thermal stress. The tree-priority route chooses the highest mean predicted adjacency among up to 30 diverse candidate paths within 1.5× shortest distance; it is not a guarantee of the globally most shaded path. Routes, crossings and entrances remain unverified. Regional summer weather is shared context rather than block-specific temperature.

Data and methods: Landsat heat

USGS Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 Level-2 surface temperature, June–August 2025. Eleven acquisition dates around 12:02 PM Central. Clouds and low-quality pixels are excluded; each composite pixel requires at least three valid dates. The delivered grid is 30 m; native thermal detail is approximately 100 m.

Block values average pixelwise summer medians. Estimates are available for 2,718 of 2,862 blocks. Small blocks without usable pixel centers remain unknown. This is surface temperature, not air temperature or daily maximum.
