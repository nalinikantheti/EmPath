Then look at the walk.

A place can be close and still be hard to reach without a car. A hot, exposed walk or a difficult crossing can stand between someone and daily groceries, community or time outdoors. EmPath helps you investigate that gap in Plano.

For people moving or considering a home

  1. Choose your places. Select the types of destinations you want nearby. We find the lowest-effort mapped option in each category.
  2. Set an effort budget. Choose 10, 20 or 30 effort minutes for each destination. The estimate includes a penalty for environmental conditions.
  3. Explore an area or address. Blue areas have lower average effort among matches. Gray areas may exceed your budget, lack a 2020 housing record, or need connection review.
  4. Inspect the route. Select a matching area, then a destination card. Check actual walking time and where the route goes.
  5. Put it in context. Compare area rent/home-value estimates, then visit the area and verify the actual entrances and crossings before making a decision.

An address lookup selects its Census area. The walking route begins at that area's reference point, not your front door. EmPath does not list available homes or determine whether a route is appropriate for a person's medical or mobility needs.

What is a walking-effort minute?

We start with estimated walking time from distance. We then add an allowance for limited tree cover, nearby traffic and hotter surfaces. For example, a 10-minute walk could count as 15 effort minutes under less favorable conditions.

The heat input summarizes summer 2025 surface temperatures; it is not live weather or air temperature. This is a comparison measure, not a prediction of how tired you will feel. Each route card shows ordinary walking time separately. The environmental factors currently have equal assumed importance.

What the route colors mean

The colored route lines distinguish destinations. The area colors compare average effort: blue is lower and red is higher among areas matching your selection. Neither color is a safety rating.

Why could an area be gray?

It may not have a route to every selected category within your budget; its connection may need review; the mapped network may be disconnected; or there may be no housing recorded in the 2020 Census. Gray does not mean nobody can walk there.

Does the city boundary cut off better options?

The search includes a three-kilometer ring beyond Plano, with environmental weights as well as roads and destinations. Public results are restricted to short walks whose effort budget is below a conservative lower bound on reaching the outer edge. That prevents an option outside the search area from unfairly beating a qualifying result under this model. It does not guarantee that every real path or business has been mapped.

Are these turn-by-turn directions?

No. The lines are the actual mapped network routes used for comparison. Entrances, barriers, continuous sidewalks, crossing controls, closures and accessibility details are not fully verified.

What happens to an address I enter?

The lookup sends the address to the U.S. Census Bureau through the site's address service to find an approximate location. EmPath does not save entered addresses. Shared links contain the area and your selected destinations, not your typed address.

For city planners and community organizations

  1. Choose a service need. Compare groceries, parks, pharmacies or other destinations rather than combining every category automatically.
  2. Locate residents and barriers. Use the population overlay and housing/context comparison to investigate populated areas with difficult modeled access.
  3. Inspect the actual routes. Look for busy-road crossings, indirect connections and paths that may need shade or maintenance. Verify the mapped conditions.
  4. Develop an intervention to test. A crossing, trail connection or shade project is a hypothesis for follow-up analysis. EmPath does not automatically prove its benefits.

Aggregate age, disability, income and vehicle availability can help evaluate who is served. They are context, not inputs that label a neighborhood as suitable for a particular demographic group.

For developers and housing organizations

Use the tool to examine access to daily services and possible pedestrian connections. Consider affordability alongside accessibility. The rent and home-value layers are Census area medians, not appraisals, current listings or forecasts.
