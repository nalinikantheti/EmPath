EmPath compares modeled environmental walking effort from Census-area reference points to everyday destinations. The production area is the official City of Plano, with roads, destinations and environmental context extending 3 km beyond its boundary.

1. Data acquisition and geometry preparation

The municipal boundary comes from City of Plano GIS. Census blocks define the origin areas. OpenStreetMap supplies the walk-mode network and most destination candidates; municipal facilities and documented curated addresses supplement the catalog. The current catalog contains 25 destination categories.

  • Spatial reference: metric operations use NAD83 / UTM zone 14N (EPSG:26914); web display uses WGS84 longitude/latitude (EPSG:4326).
  • Geometry cleaning: invalid polygon geometries are repaired, intersected with the declared study geometry, and restricted to positive-area polygon portions. Spatial indexes accelerate intersections and nearest-feature joins.
  • Network extraction: OSMnx builds a simplified directed walk graph while retaining disconnected components. OSM walk-mode coverage is not a verified inventory of continuous sidewalks or accessible entrances.
  • Destination classification: OSM tags and documented municipal/curated records assign service categories. Deterministic identifiers and deduplication preserve provenance.
  • Reference points: interior representative points are used for origin polygons and mapped destinations. SciPy cKDTree nearest-neighbor queries snap these positions to original graph junctions. Origin and destination snap distances are retained; gaps over 100 m are flagged.

Snapping does not create a verified entrance or a walkable connector. Connector travel, entrance barriers and crossing waits are excluded from the route cost.

2. AI tree-cover prediction

The AI component is pretrained semantic segmentation using Restor TCD restor/tcd-segformer-mit-b0 (SegFormer-B0) on 2022 USDA NAIP RGB aerial imagery at 0.6 m resolution. EmPath does not train a local route-choice model.

  1. Reproject and tile the imagery on a common metric grid. The inference plan uses 896-pixel cores with 64-pixel halos to reduce tile-edge effects.
  2. Run the pretrained model, bilinearly resize its output logits to the input grid, apply softmax, and classify a pixel as tree where the tree probability is at least 0.5.
  3. Trim the halos, mask missing or out-of-scope pixels, and mosaic the core predictions. Compatible cached class predictions are reused; unavailable probabilities remain unknown.
  4. Rasterize Census-block labels and aggregate pixel counts: canopy fraction = predicted tree pixels / valid observed pixels. Coverage is stored separately from the tree fraction.

The expanded production run covers 1,524 tiles, combining 823 compatible cached tiles and 701 new predictions. The model's training imagery was finer than NAIP, so local transfer accuracy requires independent validation. Predicted tree cover estimates vegetation presence; it does not directly measure shade on a sidewalk at a particular hour.

4. Transferring environmental factors to edges

Each walking edge intersects Census environmental polygons. For each factor, EmPath calculates a positive-intersection-length-weighted mean. Zero-length touches contribute no weight. When no valid intersected-block value exists, a nearest valid block within 250 m supplies a flagged proxy; equal-distance ties are resolved by block ID. Partial coverage below 98% is flagged.

w_e = (L_e / 1.2) × [1 + ((1 − C_e) + Q_e + H_e) / 3]
  • w_e: weighted edge cost in equivalent seconds.
  • L_e: mapped edge length in meters; 1.2 m/s is the assumed walking speed.
  • C_e: estimated canopy fraction; Q_e: traffic burden; H_e: heat burden, each normalized to 0–1.

All three factor estimates are required. Unknown-weight edges are excluded rather than given a default cost. The current production export has finite weights for all 290,552 edges. The multiplier lies between 1 and 2. Equal factor weights and penalty strength are explicit assumptions, rather than parameters learned from local walking preferences. Block overlays can smooth conditions along individual paths.

5. Category search and displayed routes

Offline category scoring: NetworkX multi-source Dijkstra runs on the reversed weighted graph, starting from the candidate destination nodes in one category. This yields the minimum directed-network cost from every reachable origin to its lowest-cost candidate in that category. Destination nodes shared by multiple candidates use deterministic representative records.

Interactive route reconstruction: the browser loads the gzip-compressed graph export and builds adjacency lists. A binary-min-heap Dijkstra search starts at the selected origin and stops after reaching the selected destination nodes. Predecessor edge indexes reconstruct the actual OSM edge geometry. Token checks cancel obsolete selections; long searches yield periodically so the interface can respond.

C_k(b) = min over destinations d in category k [min over paths P from b to d Σ(w_e)] / 60

Displayed route costs are checked against cached category costs within 0.02 equivalent minutes. Ordinary walking time is calculated separately as route length / 1.2 m/s. Route lines distinguish destination categories. Removing a displayed route changes visibility while preserving its computed cost and destination selection.

6. Area comparison, suggestions and map colors

A(b) = (C_1(b) + C_2(b) + … + C_K(b)) / K
Display score = 100 × clip((A(b) − P2) / (P98 − P2), 0, 1)

Each selected category contributes one lowest-cost trip with equal weight. If any selected category is unreachable, the combined mean is unknown; missing trips never count as zero. No selected categories produces no combined comparison.

P2 and P98 are the 2nd and 98th percentiles of complete area means for the current selection. Colors rescale when categories change: blue represents lower relative effort and red higher relative effort. A uniform valid distribution receives a display score of zero. Gray indicates incomplete selected-category route data.

The map displays all supported Plano areas without a walking-budget cutoff. Suggested residential areas additionally require positive 2020 Census housing and an origin snap gap ≤100 m. Eligible candidates sort by mean effort, with area ID as a deterministic tie-breaker; the sidebar shows the first ten. These suggestion filters do not exclude valid areas from the color map.

7. Search-extent checks

Roads, destinations and environmental weights extend together into a 3 km buffer beyond Plano. The lower bound for reaching the outer edge from an actual snapped origin is:

exit_bound_minutes = 0.99 × projected_distance_to_outer_boundary / 1.2 / 60

Every weighted edge costs at least its ordinary walking-time cost. Therefore, a candidate or detour outside the search extent cannot improve a known route below this bound. The factor 0.99 conservatively allows for projection differences; source edge-length ratios were checked. The minimum eligible origin bound is approximately 40.6 minutes.

Longer routes remain visible, with a coverage note when a selected route reaches or exceeds its origin-specific bound. The bound qualifies search extent under the fixed graph and cost model; it does not establish complete real-world path or destination coverage.

8. Population, housing and address lookup

Population density is 2020 Census population divided by full-block land area. City-border fragments are flagged rather than assigned redistributed population. Housing and demographic overlays use 2020–2024 ACS five-year estimates with 90% margins of error: block groups for most fields and Census tracts for disability. Geometry clipping does not redistribute whole-area estimates. Rent and home-value medians are contextual estimates rather than current property listings.

The context overlay uses a separate purple 2nd–98th percentile color stretch and does not enter route weights or the ranking. Population–access comparisons aggregate eligible residential origins and require at least five per block group; they are descriptive ecological comparisons.

Optional address lookup sends a one-line address through the server-side address service to the U.S. Census geocoder. A ray-casting point-in-ring test assigns the result to a Polygon or MultiPolygon Census area, accounting for holes. Routing still begins at that area's reference junction. Typed addresses are not saved by the application; shared links contain categories and area ID.

9. Evaluation, uncertainty and reproduction

  • Finite, nonnegative edge weights and the documented formula are checked against exported costs.
  • Reverse multi-source category costs are spot-checked against forward Dijkstra searches; displayed routes are checked against cached costs.
  • Time-only and friction-weighted rankings are compared on the same supported graph. Residential essential-profile rank correlation is approximately 0.994, with 19 of the top 20 origins shared.
  • 60/20/20 factor-priority scenarios test sensitivity to canopy, traffic and heat emphasis. These research profiles include temple, grocery and pharmacy categories; the public starting preset is grocery, pharmacy and park.
  • A same-destination benchmark compares six origins and three services: 18 pairs, with 12 using different edges under friction weighting. These computational checks are not measured resident preference accuracy.
  • Route visibility, category changes, missing selections, address-to-area lookup, sharing, keyboard controls and mobile layout are checked separately from the model.

Coverage is not prediction accuracy. Local canopy labels, observed walking preferences, verified entrances, crossing controls, closures, continuous sidewalks and personal accessibility remain incomplete. Surface-temperature vintage, canopy vintage and Census vintage differ. The application supports investigation rather than certifying safety, comfort, health outcomes or individual property suitability.
