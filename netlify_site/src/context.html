Does this duplicate walking access?

Walking comparison uses the grocery, pharmacy and park profile. Lower walking cost means easier modeled access. This is a descriptive association, not causation or individual suitability.

How to read these layers

  • Population: 2020 Census residents per square kilometer of original block land. This shows where people lived, not current migration.
  • Rent and home value: 2020–2024 ACS medians, not current listings or sale prices. Popups show available 90% margins of error.
  • Demographics: area-level estimates; they do not describe every household or change a recommendation.
  • Different boundaries: density uses 2020 blocks; most ACS context uses 2024 block groups; disability uses 2024 tracts. No invented block-level precision.

Who can use this?

  • Households: inspect routes to personally chosen services and consider area-level affordability.
  • City planners: identify populated areas with difficult access; investigate crossings, missing connections and shade.
  • Developers and housing organizations: evaluate pedestrian connections and affordable access to services.

Good walking access should be available without forcing a household to own a car. EmPath can help locate barriers and compare potential improvements; it does not measure health outcomes or predict property-price changes.
