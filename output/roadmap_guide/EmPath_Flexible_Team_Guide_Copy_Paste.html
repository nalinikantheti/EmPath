EmPath Team Work Guide

A flexible guide for dividing work and keeping the prototype moving. Adjust responsibilities as needs and availability change.

Shared Goal

  • Build a Python-based demo comparing distance-only and environmental-friction walking access across Plano ZIP areas 75025, 75024, 75023, and 75074.

Nalini Leads Implementation and Storytelling

  • Prepare the data and walking network.
  • Connect the GeoAI component to route scoring and residential rankings.
  • Build the interactive map and explain the project through the final presentation.

Pramukh Leads GIS and Data Science Support

  • Review data coverage, network connections, and geographic assumptions.
  • Help adapt the existing model and evaluate whether results make sense.
  • Troubleshoot priority issues and help check the final demo.

Work in Three Broad Phases

  • Prepare: organize data, agree on a feasible approach, and get the basic pipeline running.
  • Build and review: Nalini connects the components while Pramukh reviews outputs and helps resolve gaps.
  • Finish: focus on one clear demonstration, explain limitations, and rehearse the presentation.

Coordinate Simply

  • Use the two-hour meeting to review progress, choose priorities, and agree on ownership.
  • Keep one shared list: task, owner, status, and blocker. Hand off saved outputs as they become available.
  • Keep the core workflow reproducible in Python. Pramukh can use GIS software for review if helpful.

Time and Priorities

  • Nalini: about 20 hours total, including the meeting and nine-hour hackathon.
  • Pramukh: about 15 hours total, including the same meeting and hackathon; schedule the remaining time flexibly.
  • Prioritize a working network, a usable GeoAI component, explainable routes, and a clear map. Reduce optional features if needed.
  • If Pramukh has extra time: check more routes, explore sensitivity to scoring assumptions, or improve the visualization.

Aim to Finish With

  • A working demo and reproducible code.
  • A concise record of sources, methods, checks, and limitations.
  • A five-minute presentation with backup screenshots.
